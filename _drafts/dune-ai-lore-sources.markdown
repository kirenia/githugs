People ask where I get this stuff. Here it is, for the Dune video, in the order it shows up on screen. Everything is Frank Herbert, and everything is checkable in a copy you can hold.

## The cover quote

> Thou shalt not make a machine in the likeness of a human mind.

That is the commandment from the Orange Catholic Bible, and it is real, printed, 1965. You can find it twice in the book, and this is the part that trips people up: chapter one has Paul say "a man's mind," and the appendix, "Terminology of the Imperium," says "human mind." Both are Herbert. They are two wordings of the same commandment, and I picked one for the card rather than blending them.

The other thing worth saying out loud: "Thou shalt not disfigure the soul" is the supreme commandment in that same religion. The machine line is the Jihad's chief commandment. People mix those two up constantly.

## Why the ban exists

Reverend Mother Mohiam explains it to Paul in chapter one, right after the gom jabbar:

> Once men turned their thinking over to machines in the hope that this would set them free. But that only permitted other men with machines to enslave them.

That is the whole thesis of the video in one sentence, written in 1965, before the microprocessor existed. She also says the Great Revolt "forced human minds to develop," which is where Mentats, Navigators and the Bene Gesserit come from.

## The dates

The glossary entry is the part nobody reads:

> JIHAD, BUTLERIAN: (see also Great Revolt) the crusade against computers, thinking machines, and conscious robots begun in 201 B.G. and concluded in 108 B.G.

Ninety-three years. That is a war that outlived everyone who started it, and Herbert's own word for it there is "crusade," which matters if you have seen my other video about that.

## The part everyone quotes wrong

Decades later, in *God Emperor of Dune* (1981), Leto II says the target of the Jihad was "a machine-attitude as much as the machines." The ban was aimed at a habit, not at hardware. If you only take one idea from the video, take that one.

## What I left out, on purpose

The detailed war, the robot armies, Serena Butler: all of that comes from the prequels by Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson, which are a separate canon. They are fine books. They are not what I am quoting when I say "Dune said." I keep those apart on camera and I will keep doing that.

Herbert also wrote about what he was building in "Dune Genesis," an essay in *Omni*, July 1980. It is short and it is him explaining the book's purpose in his own words, which is about as primary as a source gets.

## Go read it

- *Dune*, Frank Herbert (1965): chapter one, and "Terminology of the Imperium" in the appendices. [Bookshop](https://bookshop.org/a/128823/9780441172719)
- *God Emperor of Dune*, Frank Herbert (1981): Leto II on the machine-attitude.
- "Dune Genesis," Frank Herbert, *Omni*, July 1980.
